# Content & Messaging Standards — Endless Customers

Companion to `site-spec.md` (structure/IA) and `visual-style-guide.md` (imagery). This file governs **what the words say and how they're framed**. Read the relevant part before writing or restructuring page copy. Distilled from recurring direction across sessions (June 2026, per Bob). Where this and the site-spec decisions log disagree on a specific page, the decisions log wins.

## 1. AI positioning is front and center

AI is the defining pressure on our buyers right now, and Endless Customers is framed as the answer to it — not a bolted-on feature, not a scattered mention.

> "AI is so critically important right now, and we need to be positioning Endless Customers as a solution to AI... that needs to be really front and center." — Bob

- The AI-era framing is a lead message on the homepage, the Core Concepts & Frameworks hub, and the service pages.
- When building or revising any major page, check: does this carry the AI framing where it belongs? If a top-of-funnel page is silent on AI, that's usually a gap.
- Position AI as the reason the system matters now, not as a separate product line.

## 2. Lead with the buyer's journey, not the system's internals

A newcomer is asking "should I care?" and "is this for me?" — not "what's in the system?" Branded framework names earn their place deeper, once interest exists.

> "Starting with the Big Five and Selling Seven and assignment selling is probably the wrong thing to do at the top." — Bob

- Top-of-page and top-of-funnel copy answers buyer questions first: should I care, is this for me, what will it do for my business, what does it cost.
- Big 5, Selling 7, Assignment Selling, Sticky 5, the Scorecard, the Pride Cycle and the rest are the *how*. They go below the *why*, after the buyer is bought in.
- "Start with these" / entry cards are buyer questions, not framework showcases.

## 3. The site speaks as Endless Customers / IMPACT — not as Marcus

Marcus's content is an input we mimic, not the voice target. This is the EC/IMPACT brand's site.

> "It needs to be the voice and tone of IMPACT and Endless Customers. We will mimic a lot of Marcus's tone, but this is the Endless Customers website, not Marcus's." — Bob

- Name files, prompts, and the planned voice guide around the EC/IMPACT brand, not around Marcus personally.
- Marcus is a primary author/face and his cadence is a strong influence, but copy should read as the brand, not as one person's blog.

## 4. CTAs ride high — don't bury them

Primary conversion actions (subscribe, book a call, get the diagnostic, get the book) default to a high page position, not the closer slot alone.

> "This page is missing a call to action to subscribe." → then immediately: "Can we move that subscription CTA higher on the page?" — Bob

- Every content page has an obvious, early conversion path, plus the closing CTA.
- High-intent words belong where they convert: e.g. "Pricing" should appear in the anchor pill / CTA, not be hidden behind softer labels.

## 5. On conversion pages, withhold detail to drive the action

When a page's job is one conversion (a download, a form fill, a call), tease rather than fully disclose, so the CTA is the only path to the detail.

> "I don't want to give away too much on the sponsors page because we want them downloading the prospectus. That's the main call to action." — Bob

- Sponsor/landing pages: show enough to create want, route the rest through the form/download.
- Decide nav and exit points with the same lens — a conversion page may warrant minimal or no nav (see site-spec §3 and the design-qa note on nav as a conversion variable).

## 6. Label things in the visitor's words

Section names and nav labels use visitor vocabulary, not internal product names.

> "I don't think 'the Library' makes the most sense. They probably want to know what the learning content is — guides or courses." — Bob

- Prefer "Courses," "Guides," "Pricing," "Success Stories" over inside-baseball names.
- Test a label against "what would a first-time visitor call this?" before shipping it.

## 7. Terminology — approved terms

Small wording corrections recur. Keep this list current; add to it whenever Bob corrects a term.

- Write **"and"**, not "&", in section names and labels.
- Use exact product names (e.g. "The Endless Customers Book," not "The Book," when the full name matters).
- **No em dashes** (also a sitewide CLAUDE.md rule) — periods, commas, or parentheses instead.
- **VERIFY — "Providers" vs "Specialists":** there is a signal that Bob prefers "Specialists" over "Providers" for the service team, but `site-spec.md` and `people.json` currently use "Service Providers" as a role. Confirm the intended term before any sweep, then record the decision here and in the decisions log.

## 8. Author attribution & E-E-A-T

Content/reference pages are built for credibility in the AI-answer era.

- Concept and article pages support multiple authors + contributors + an editor, each with photo/title/bio; most will carry a video.
- Cross-link to the editorial process / standards where it reinforces trust.
- This is both a design requirement (the byline/credits treatment) and an SEO/E-E-A-T requirement. See the concept-page decision in the site-spec decisions log for the built treatment.
